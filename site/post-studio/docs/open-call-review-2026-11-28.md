# Open calls on Instagram: review, 28 Nov 2026

Sources:
- @artstoheartsproject: 197 posts, 29 Sep–27 Nov.
- @opencallforartists_: 150 posts, 20 Oct–27 Nov.
- @opencallsbyathproject: 7 posts. The account was dormant from Sep 2025 until 23 Nov.
- Peer research: Jackson's, Aesthetica, LensCulture, Booooooom, Studio Visit, Create!, RA, Lumen.

Only likes and comments are available (no reach or saves). On open-call posts, comments are mostly keyword requests, so comments are the conversion number.

## Verdict
The calls that convert lead with **the offer**: money, print, a theme people love. They also carry **one keyword**.

Everything after launch day repeats the launch with a smaller hook, so it dies: Garden went 621 → 116 → 15 → 10 comments.

The studio's own new short formats are now the worst performers on the account:
- "Get your work on our shelf": 2 comments
- "Here is the page it became": 9
- Garden FAQ: 10
- "Get published in the Garden Edition": 15

## What the data says

**1. The offer in the first line converts.**
- "Artists, $500 to support your creative practice." got **1,441** comments (Creative Fund).
- "OPEN CALL: WOMAN ARTIST AWARD – 7TH EDITION" with $500 in the caption got 240.
- On @opencallforartists_, "🌸 FLOWERS THROUGH THE EYES OF ART" got **702** and "Calling all artists: Birds & the Art of Nature is open for entries" got **408**.
- What these share: a broad, loved subject or a sum of money, said plainly.
- What fails: short captions with no offer, and organisation-first openings.

**2. No keyword means no conversions.**
- @opencallforartists_ posts without a keyword: median **1** comment, against 38 with one.
- Artist of the Year ran 5 posts with no keyword, then switched to "AWARD", which the Woman Artist Award was using at the same time, then to "2026" (2 comments).
- The keyword sits in the first two lines in **1 of 31** open-call posts.

**3. Repeating the same post kills it.**
- Birds was posted 11 times: 311, 408, 254, 62, 55, 113, 16, 0 … comments.
- The Artist of the Year caption was used 5 times.
- About 40 of 150 @opencallforartists_ posts reuse a caption body word for word.
- Countdown reposts ("LAST/FINAL/Ending soon") get a median 17 comments against 36.5.

**4. Real urgency with a keyword works; template countdowns don't.**
- Working: "FINAL 72 HOURS" (140 comments), "ENDS IN 2 WEEKS" (108), "5 days left… comment" (82).
- Not working: "Halfway. 17 days left" (7).

**5. Fee in the caption.**
- On @opencallforartists_, third-party posts that state a fee got a median **0** comments, and "free" ones got 64.5.
- ATH's own $12 calls still won on their first post.
- So the fee belongs in the FAQ and the application page, not in every caption.

**6. @opencallforartists_ sells ATH's own calls better than the main account does.**
- Woman Artist Award there: **910 likes / 273 comments** ("Dm 'APPLY'"), against 240 on the main account.
- Artist of the Year there had no keyword: 37/6, 38/2, 100/5.
- Every call should get a version written for that account.

**7. Reels and features reach far.**
- A featured-artist reel (Dinah Letteboer, 101 Colors) got 11,555 likes.
- Open calls have had one reel in 60 days (7 comments).
- Quotes get huge likes and convert nothing.

## Peers (what serious calls do)
- They name the juror and the institution: Jackson's lists its guest judges; Studio Visit names its curator and the museum.
- They state the prize exactly and what it buys.
- They name the publication as the prize: Aesthetica's book, Booooooom's zine.
- They show real past winners and their work.
- Mid-call they run **juror introductions** (Booooooom, "one more month"), past-winner stories, and "what the selector looks for".
- Reminders go out at one week, three days and the last day, not every second day.
- Human copy uses proper nouns and facts: names, dates, titles, the artist's own words. It avoids "elevate / journey / unlock / not just X, it's Y" and rule-of-three lines.
- Instagram:
  - "Sends" are now the strongest reach signal, so give people a reason to forward a post ("send this to someone who paints flowers").
  - When someone skips a carousel, Instagram may show it again starting at slide 2, so slide 2 must also work as a cover.
  - 7–10 slides is the sweet spot.
  - Captions are searchable, and Google indexes them, so the first line works as a headline.

## The plan: every call gets its own campaign

**1. Fewer posts, each with a new reason to comment.** About 8 feed posts per call instead of 15, built from that call's own material:

| When | Post | Its own hook |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Launch | The offer, stated like the Fund post: money / print / subject |
| Day 3 | The theme | Real artworks that fit, "what we want to see" (the post that got 116) |
| Day 6 | Who decides | Juror/curator name and words; or past cover artists |
| Day 9 | Proof | One real past artist: the work, the printed page, their words |
| Day 13 | Practical | How to photograph / five mistakes. Built to be saved |
| −14 | 2 weeks left | Offer recap + keyword (the "ENDS IN 2 WEEKS" format, 108 comments) |
| −5 | 5 days left | Short, keyword in line 2 (82 comments) |
| −3 / 0 | 72 hours / last day | One slide, one action |

Plus one reel per call (a past artist's page flipping, or the juror) and an @opencallforartists_ version of the launch.

**2. Every call reads differently.** The studio picks the launch angle from the call's strongest asset:
- Fund-type calls: money first.
- Themed books (Garden, Flowers): the subject first.
- Magazine: the issue and the anniversary.
- Award calls: the title.

Hook lines come from a bank and rotate per call, so two calls never open the same way.

**3. Keyword rules, enforced by the studio.**
- One keyword per call. The Call manager already locks it.
- It goes in caption line 1 or 2, on the cover strip, and on the last slide.
- A keyword can never be reused across live calls.

**4. Copy rules.**
- Names, numbers, dates, the artist's own words.
- No triplets, no "not just… it's…", no "elevate/journey/unlock".
- The fee goes in the FAQ and the DM, not in every caption.

- The fee has been taken out of the facts line.
