      The rest of the series

      The book you are reading is pulled forward.

       

      Creative brief · ATH website redesign · Foundation

      Arts to Hearts is a house. This is its bookshop.

      ATH is becoming an art centre you can walk through: a gallery, an artist’s studio, a bookshop, a café, a reading room and the open calls that bring new artists in. Each product is a room. Every room shares one front door, one corridor and one way out, so the whole house feels like one place. The Bookshop is the first room we build on that foundation.

      The feeling

      Walking into a good bookshop

      You step in from the street and the noise drops. Spines line the walls. A few books lie open on a low table, face-up, with their best spread showing. You pick one up, feel the weight, turn a page, and hear it. A handwritten card from the bookseller says why this one matters.

      That is the experience we design for. Images lead, because books are about images. The pages turn and sound like pages. The artists speak to you from their own books. Nothing on screen asks for more than one decision at a time, and every room has a lot of air in it.

      The old Editions pages showed the same content in five places, in many type styles, with no order to the visit. The fix is a story with a beginning, a middle and an end, told with the objects of a bookshop.

      Who walks in

      Three visitors, one action each

      • The collectorWants a beautiful object and the story behind it. Leaves with a book in the bag.
      • The artistWants to see what being in the book looks like. Leaves with an application to the next open call.
      • The readerCurious about artists and how they work. Leaves having listened to an artist and kept a page in their notes, and comes back.

      The visit

      Scrolling is walking in

      1. i.The windowOne book, face out, under a soft light. One sentence. One way in.
      2. ii.The shelvesEach series is a shelf of spines. Lift one to read it, choose it to open the book. The empty slot is the next open call.
      3. iii.The tableThe book of the month lies open. Drag the pages, hear them turn, see the stack beside it.
      4. iv.The artistA voice note and its words, read along as the artist speaks. A card in their own words.
      5. v.The counterA side drawer, not a new page. Price, one button, done.
      6. vi.The doorThe footer: the books you looked at stack up, and the house name says goodbye.

      The senses

      What makes it feel real

      • SightFull-bleed images and spreads. Real covers, real spines, real studio photographs. Never stock photos, never lorem ipsum.
      • TouchSpines lift. Covers tilt toward the pointer. Pages follow a drag. Stacks shift when you reach for the top book.
      • SoundOff until the visitor turns it on. Then: a page turn, a book sliding off a shelf, a soft stack thud. Nothing loops, nothing plays on its own.
      • VoiceArtists speak in short voice notes, with the transcript shown so nobody needs headphones.
      • PaperWarm paper ground and a faint fibre texture on covers and pages. It stands in for the smell of books.

      Shared parts

      The house infrastructure

      arts to hearts
      Floating menuA solid ballet-pink bar that floats 14px below the top edge. Logo, six links, Sound, Notes, Bag and Menu. It tucks away as you scroll down and returns when you scroll up. On phones it keeps the logo, Bag and Menu.
      Side drawersOne drawer that slides in from the right for every side task. Menu shows the rooms of the house. Notes keeps pages you saved. Bag is the counter. Each closes with Esc, the scrim or Close.
      arts to hearts
      FooterBurgundy with the scalloped edge. A stack of books settles one by one as you arrive, then the house name runs edge to edge. Link columns stay short.
      Bookshop objectsCover, spine, shelf, stack, open book, note card, scalloped frame, voice note. Every Bookshop page is made from these and nothing else.

      Guardrails

      Rules every page keeps

      1. One message per screen. One headline, one line, one action.
      2. Breathe. At least 160px between sections on desktop and 96px on phones.
      3. Images lead. Text sits beside an image, never on top of a busy one.
      4. Real objects only. Covers, spines, stacks and pages. No sports podiums, no badges.
      5. Fixed words. Add to bag. Look inside. Keep this page. Apply by date.
      6. Motion is physical. Lift, settle, turn. Under 600ms, one bigger moment per page.
      7. Sound is opt-in. Off by default, remembered once chosen.
      8. Square corners, softly. 12px radius. Never pill buttons.
      9. Same parts everywhere. Menu, drawers and footer never change between rooms.
      10. Phones first-class. Shelves scale, drawers become full-screen, pages still turn.
