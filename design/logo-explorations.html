Arts to Hearts · Logo explorations · Round 1

Same name, same shape. Cleaner, warmer, ready for books.

Every direction keeps what people already know: lowercase “arts to hearts”, the small italic “to”, “project” underneath, pink on chilli red. The changes are in spacing, in which face sets the “to”, and in one optional detail. Each direction is shown large, on the four brand grounds, and at real sizes: the website menu, a book spine and a favicon.

What staysLowercase name, italic “to”, the word “project” below, Chilli, Burgundy, Peony and Cream.
What we fixUneven spacing around “to”, “project” floating loose, and the script “project” that reads as a font default.
How to judgeLook at the menu bar and the 16px favicon first. A logo that only works big is not finished.

Monogram

ATH for small spaces

For the avatar, favicon, seal and book spines. The italic “t” is the same idea as the italic “to”, so the monogram reads as part of the family. The scalloped seal reuses the scallop edge from the footer. Below 32px the three letters blur, so the favicon is the italic “t” alone.

At working size

Recommendation

Go with B, and use the italic t monogram for small spaces.

  1. B keeps the logo you have and sets the “to” in Instrument Serif italic, the same face as every website headline. Logo and site now speak with one voice.
  2. E is B plus a heart dot. It matches the Chilli heart dot on every brand icon. Try it on merch and the book spine before deciding.
  3. Drop the script “project”. Spaced capitals hold up at 16px; the script does not.
  4. D follows the brand guide’s own suggestion (Faculty Glyphic and Playfair). It is the most bookish, but the furthest from the logo people know.
  5. Next: pick two, test in foil on a cover, and redraw the chosen one as vectors with proper kerning. These boards use web fonts as stand-ins.
