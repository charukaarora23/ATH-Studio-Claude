## 1. Technical: how they are built

**Silhouette and layering**
- Every convincing folder has 3 planes: back board, contents, front board. Contents always break the top edge (7, 8, 25, 26). Ours does this; v2 should let contents be more varied (slips of different heights and tints, not identical sheets).
- Tabs are cut from the board, never stuck on: top tabs (8, 11, 26, 27), side tabs with rotated labels (12, 13, 19, 28), and a bottom pull-tab (28). Tabs carry navigation text; they are UI.
- A thumb cut or scoop (8, 25) explains how you reach inside.

**Light and depth**
- One light source, soft contact shadows, no outlines. Depth comes from overlap and a 1–2° rotation, not from bevels.
- Dark grounds (5, 10, 14, 19, 22, 25, 27) make paper glow. Light grounds (8, 9, 16) rely on colour blocks instead.

**Texture**
- Fine grain on everything (5, 15, 25, 26), visible paper fibre on kraft (9, 12, 13). Texture is low-contrast and never sits on body text in a way that hurts reading.

**Typography**
- Big serif display with an italic word swap is the most repeated move (1, 2, 16, 20, 24, 26, 28). Sans for UI, mono for metadata (9, 12, 19, 22). Handwriting only for filled-in answers or signatures (5, 13, 14, 15).
- Big numerals for counts (16, 22, 14 percentages).

**Information structure**
- Record cards: labelled cells filled with content (5, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15). Ruled tables with hairlines and mono labels.
- Numbered sections (11, 18, 22), page counters (3, 4).

**Fasteners**
- Paperclips, binder clips and badge clips are drawn with real metal highlights and always hold something (a photo, a label, a receipt). Never decorative floaters.

**Colour mechanics**
- One saturated field per composition (orange band 22, red band 23, cobalt folder 19, Burgundy half 16, Chilli photo 20) against neutral paper.
- Secondary tints applied to *objects*, not backgrounds: pink envelope, lemon receipt, lavender pills, sage sheet.

## 2. Creative: what they are saying

- **The archive as identity.** Folders, IDs, record cards and certificates say "this is your file". For an artist bio tool this is exactly the promise: your professional identity, kept and versioned.
- **Collage, but edited.** The strongest ones (8, 9, 19, 26, 28) use 3–5 objects with clear hierarchy. The weaker collage ones (17, 18) scatter decoration; take the craft, not the clutter.
- **Editorial confidence.** Huge type, generous space, one bold field. This is ATH's "space is luxury" and "one red leads".
- **Analogue + digital hybrid.** Browser chrome on paper (18), Instagram UI on a portfolio (28), cursor on a card (24). The interface itself can be a material object.
- **Index as navigation.** 16, 11, 27 and 28 prove tabs can be the whole menu.

## 3. Psychological: why they work on people

- **Containment reduces anxiety.** Writing a bio triggers impostor feelings. A folder is a bounded, private container; a form with labelled boxes replaces the blank page with small answerable prompts (record cards 5, 15).
- **Ownership and endowment.** Physical-feeling objects with your name, date and number feel like *yours* and worth keeping (ID card 9, archive card 5). Filing a version becomes a small ritual of completion.
- **Legitimacy cues.** Stamps, IDs, numbered editions and tables borrow institutional trust. Use with care: our date mark must say "saved", never "verified".
- **Human authorship.** Handwritten fills signal that a person, not a machine, wrote this. For AI-assisted writing that matters: the artist's own notes should look like their marks, the draft like typeset output.
- **Progress through objects.** Tabs, page counts and stacked sheets give a felt sense of how far along you are without a percentage bar.
- **Warmth with control.** Saturated red fields create energy and emotional warmth; neutral paper keeps it calm. The balance reads premium rather than playful.
- **Curiosity gaps.** Contents peeking from a folder (7, 25, 26) invite opening. That is our closed-folder state.

## 4. What changes in My Studio v2

1. **Chilli leads.** Bio folder in Chilli (Ballet type), statement in Burgundy (Peony), My files in Peony (Burgundy). Inside of the open cover is a full Chilli field.
2. **Secondary tints on objects.** Each chapter tab owns a tint (Ballet, Lemon, Pistachio, Cool Blue, Hot Rose); notes cards, version slips and receipts pick them up. Never more than one tint per object.
3. **Serif display with italic swap** for page questions and folder titles (Newsreader), Hanken for UI, DM Mono for record fields.
4. **Record-card everything:** notes, facts used and the filed version become ruled cards with mono labels and the artist's answers set in Newsreader italic.
5. **Functional clips:** one clip per screen, holding the latest version slip or the filing card.
6. **Big numerals:** word count "104/150", versions "04".
7. **Contrast moment per screen:** one large Chilli or Burgundy field; optional Ink desk mode for dark-ground drama.
8. **Coloured top-tab index** on the studio home (from 16, 27) for Bio / Statement / My files / Settings.
9. **Translucent sleeve** for filed documents in My files (14, 25).

Guardrails kept from the ATH guide: one red leads, Chilli and Orange never touch, type always in the tonal partner, no stickers or star bursts, logo untouched.
